Glossary (A–Z)

Every key term from across the notes, in one place — skim it, or use Ctrl/Cmd-F to search.

A

TermMeaning
AMC (Asset Management Company)The company that runs a mutual fund and decides where your pooled money is invested.
Annual reportA yearly document a company publishes showing its profits, debts and plans, useful before you invest.
Ask priceThe lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept for a share.

B

TermMeaning
Bear marketA long stretch when share prices are generally falling and investors feel gloomy.
Bid priceThe highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay for a share.
Blue-chipA large, well-known and financially strong company, such as those in the Nifty 50.
Bonus sharesExtra free shares a company gives existing shareholders out of its reserves.
Breakeven (options)The level the underlying must cross for a bought option to profit: strike + premium for a call, strike − premium for a put. See Options Explained.
BrokerA SEBI-registered firm (like Zerodha or Groww) through which you buy and sell shares.
Bull marketA long stretch when share prices are generally rising and investors feel confident.
BuybackWhen a company buys back its own shares from the market, usually returning cash to shareholders.

C

TermMeaning
Call optionA contract giving you the right (not obligation) to buy a share at a fixed price by a set date.
CandlestickA small chart shape showing a share's open, high, low and close price for a period.
Capital gainThe profit you make when you sell an investment for more than you paid for it.
Cash settlementSettling a derivative by paying the cash difference instead of delivering shares — how index F&O settle at expiry. See Expiry Explained.
CDSLOne of India's two depositories that hold your shares safely in electronic (demat) form.
CNC / MIS / NRMLZerodha's product tags when you place an order: CNC for delivery (shares you keep), MIS for intraday (closed the same day), NRML for carrying F&O overnight. See Using Zerodha.
CompoundingEarning returns on your past returns, so your money grows faster the longer you stay invested.
Corporate actionAny company event that affects its shares, such as dividends, bonus shares or a stock split.

D

TermMeaning
Demat accountAn account that holds your shares in electronic form, the way a bank holds your money.
DerivativeA contract whose value comes from an underlying asset like a share or index, such as futures and options.
DiversificationSpreading money across many shares or sectors so one bad pick does not sink everything.
DividendA share of a company's profit paid out in cash to its shareholders.

E

TermMeaning
ELSSAn Equity Linked Savings Scheme, a tax-saving mutual fund with a 3-year lock-in under Section 80C.
EquityAnother word for shares, meaning part-ownership in a company.
Exit loadA small fee some mutual funds charge if you withdraw your money too soon.
Expense ratioThe yearly fee a mutual fund charges, shown as a small percentage of your invested amount.
ExpiryThe fixed date a futures or options contract settles and ceases to exist. Index options expire weekly; the last weekly each month is the monthly expiry. Shares you own in delivery never expire. See Expiry Explained.

F

TermMeaning
Face valueThe original nominal value of a share set by the company, often ₹10 or ₹1.
FII / DIIForeign and Domestic Institutional Investors — big players whose buying or selling moves the market.
Fundamental analysisJudging a share by studying the company's business, profits and financial health.
FuturesA contract to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a future date.

H

TermMeaning
HedgingTaking a protective position to reduce the risk of losses on another investment.
HoldingsShares that have settled into your demat account and are yours to keep, shown separately from open intraday/F&O Positions in your broker app. See Using Zerodha.

I

TermMeaning
IndexA basket of selected shares used to track how the overall market is doing, like the Nifty 50 or Sensex.
Index fundA low-cost mutual fund that simply copies an index instead of trying to beat it.
Intrinsic value (options)The "real" part of an option's premium — how far it is in the money right now (zero for OTM options).
IPOAn Initial Public Offering, when a company sells its shares to the public for the first time.
ITM / ATM / OTMMoneyness — whether a strike is In, At or Out of The Money versus the current price. See Options Explained.

K

TermMeaning
KYCKnow Your Customer — the one-time identity check (PAN, Aadhaar) needed before you can invest.

L

TermMeaning
Large-cap / Mid-cap / Small-capCompany size groups by market value, from the biggest and steadiest to smaller and riskier.
LeverageUsing borrowed money to invest more than you have, which magnifies both gains and losses.
Limit orderAn instruction to buy or sell only at a price you set, or better.
LiquidityHow easily a share can be bought or sold quickly without moving its price much.
Lock-in periodA fixed time during which you cannot sell or withdraw an investment, as with ELSS funds.
Long-dated optionsIndex options with quarter-end or half-year expiries stretching years out; they exist but trade thinly in India.
Lot sizeThe fixed number of units you must trade together in futures and options contracts. See Options Explained.
LTCG (Long-Term Capital Gains)Tax on profit from shares or equity funds held over one year.

M

TermMeaning
MarginMoney you put up to borrow from your broker so you can take a larger position.
Market capitalisationA company's total value, found by multiplying its share price by the number of shares.
Market orderAn instruction to buy or sell immediately at the best price available right now.
Mutual fundA pool where many investors' money is managed together by professionals for a fee.

N

TermMeaning
NAV (Net Asset Value)The per-unit price of a mutual fund, updated once at the end of each trading day.
Nifty 50The index of 50 large companies on the NSE that tracks the broad Indian market.
NSDLOne of India's two depositories that hold shares electronically, alongside CDSL.
NSE / BSEIndia's two main stock exchanges — the National and Bombay Stock Exchanges where shares trade.

O

TermMeaning
Open interest (OI)The number of F&O contracts currently open at a strike — a gauge of how busy and easy to trade it is.
OptionA contract giving the right, but not the duty, to buy or sell a share at a set price by a set date.
Option chainThe table of all strikes for one expiry — calls on one side, puts on the other, strikes down the middle. See Options Explained.
Option writerThe seller of an option, who collects the premium and takes on the obligation — a small capped gain against a potentially huge loss.

P

TermMeaning
P/E ratioThe price-to-earnings ratio, showing how much you pay for each ₹1 of a company's yearly profit.
Physical settlementSettling a stock F&O contract held to expiry with actual shares instead of cash — a whole lot's worth, so it needs serious money. See Expiry Explained.
PositionsTrades that are open but not yet settled — today's intraday trades and open F&O — shown separately from your settled Holdings. See Using Zerodha.
PortfolioThe full collection of all the investments you hold.
Premium (options)The price of an option itself, quoted per unit — you pay premium × lot size. See Options Explained.
Primary marketWhere companies sell brand-new shares to the public, mainly through IPOs.
Put optionA contract giving you the right (not obligation) to sell a share at a fixed price by a set date.

R

TermMeaning
RebalancingAdjusting your holdings now and then to return to your intended mix of investments.
ROE (Return on Equity)A measure of how much profit a company makes from shareholders' money.

S

TermMeaning
SEBIThe Securities and Exchange Board of India, the regulator that protects investors and oversees the market.
Secondary marketWhere investors buy and sell already-issued shares among themselves, on the NSE and BSE.
SensexThe BSE's index of 30 large, established companies that tracks the Indian market.
SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)Investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month to build wealth steadily.
Square offClosing an open position — selling what you bought, or buying back what you sold.
STCG (Short-Term Capital Gains)Tax on profit from shares or equity funds held for one year or less.
Stop-lossAn order that automatically sells a share if its price falls to a level you set, limiting losses.
STT (Securities Transaction Tax)A small tax charged by the government on every buy and sell of shares.
Strike priceThe fixed price at which an option lets you buy or sell the underlying share.

T

TermMeaning
Technical analysisJudging a share by studying its price charts and patterns rather than the company itself.
Term insurancePure life cover that pays your family if you die during the policy term, with no investment mixed in.
Time decay (theta)The daily loss of an option's time value as expiry approaches — it accelerates sharply near the end.
Time value (options)The "hope" part of a premium, paid for the time left on the clock; it melts to zero by expiry. See Options Explained.
Trading accountThe account through which you place buy and sell orders, linked to your demat and bank accounts.

U

TermMeaning
ULIPA Unit Linked Insurance Plan that mixes life insurance with investment, often with high charges.
UnderlyingThe asset a derivative's value is based on — a stock, or an index like the Nifty.

V

TermMeaning
VolatilityHow much and how quickly a share's price swings up and down.
VolumeThe number of shares traded in a given period, showing how active a stock is.

Z

TermMeaning
Zero-DTE (0DTE)An option on its own expiry day ("zero days to expiry") — the cheapest, fastest and riskiest hours of its life. See Expiry Explained.
