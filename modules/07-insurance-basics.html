Module 7 · Insurance Basics

Insurance is the safety net under your money plan — it keeps one big emergency from undoing years of patient saving.

1. Why insurance comes first

Before you worry about which mutual fund to pick or how to beat the market, you need to protect what you already have. Insurance is simply a deal: you pay a small amount regularly (called a premium), and in return the insurance company agrees to pay a large amount if something bad happens.

Think of it as the foundation of a house. Investments are the rooms you build on top — but if the foundation cracks, everything above it falls. A single big emergency, like a long hospital stay or the sudden loss of the family's income, can wipe out years of careful saving in a matter of weeks.

THE BIG IDEA

Buy protection before you chase returns. A 12% return on your investments means nothing if one unexpected ₹8 lakh hospital bill forces you to sell everything you own.

Eg: Riya saves diligently for five years and builds ₹6 lakh. Then a family member needs surgery costing ₹7 lakh. Without health insurance, her entire savings vanish — and she still falls into debt. With a health policy, the insurer pays the bill and her savings stay untouched.

2. Term life insurance

Term life insurance is pure life cover and nothing else. You choose a term (a number of years, say until you are 60) and a cover amount (the sum your family receives). If you die during that term, your family gets the full sum. If you survive the term — which is the happy outcome — you get nothing back.

That "nothing back" is exactly why term insurance is so cheap. The company is only paying out in the rare case of death, so it can offer a huge cover for a small premium.

FeatureWhat it means
Cover (sum assured)The lump sum your family receives, Eg: ₹1 crore
TermHow long the cover lasts, Eg: 30 years
PremiumWhat you pay, often just a few thousand rupees a year for young, healthy people
Payout if you surviveNone — and that is normal and fine

A common rule of thumb is to take cover of about 10–15 times your annual income. So if you earn ₹8 lakh a year, a cover of roughly ₹80 lakh to ₹1.2 crore is a sensible starting point. This gives your family enough to replace your income for many years.

DO YOU EVEN NEED IT?

Term insurance is only needed if someone depends on you financially — a spouse, children, or parents. If nobody relies on your income, you can skip life cover for now and focus on health insurance instead.

3. Health insurance

Health insurance covers your hospital bills. When you are admitted to hospital, the insurer pays the cost (up to your sum insured, the maximum it will cover in a year) instead of you paying out of pocket.

A family floater is a single policy that covers your whole family — you, your spouse and children — under one shared sum insured. It is usually cheaper than buying separate policies for each person.

A common mistake is thinking "I'm young and healthy, so I don't need it." But hospital bills in India are large and rising, and an accident or sudden illness can happen at any age. Better still, premiums are cheapest when you are young and healthy — and once you buy early, you avoid being refused later for conditions you develop with age.

When choosing a policy, check these four things carefully:

  • Sum insured — is it enough? In a metro city, ₹5–10 lakh is a reasonable floor for a family.
  • Network hospitals — hospitals where you get cashless treatment (the insurer settles the bill directly). Make sure good ones near you are on the list.
  • Room-rent limits — some policies cap how much they pay per day for your hospital room; a low cap can leave you paying a big chunk yourself.
  • Waiting periods — the time before certain illnesses (or pre-existing conditions) are covered, often 2–4 years.

4. Insurance is NOT an investment

This is the single most important lesson in this module. Agents often try to sell "policies" that promise to both insure you and grow your money. These go by names like ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans), endowment plans and money-back policies.

AVOID MIXING THE TWO

Products that bundle insurance with investing usually do both jobs poorly: a small life cover, low returns, and high hidden charges. You end up under-insured and under-invested at the same time.

The smarter approach is to keep the two separate:

  • For protection → buy a pure term plan (large cover, low cost).
  • For growth → invest in mutual funds (see Module 5).

This way you get a much bigger life cover for far less money, and your investments grow without being dragged down by insurance charges. Whenever someone says a policy will "give you returns," treat that as a signal to slow down and look closely.

5. Other useful covers

Beyond life and health, a few other covers are worth knowing:

  • Motor insurance — legally required in India for any car or two-wheeler; covers damage and harm caused on the road.
  • Personal accident cover — pays out if an accident leads to disability or death, helping when you can no longer earn.
  • Home insurance — protects your house and belongings against fire, theft and natural disasters.

6. A simple checklist

If this feels like a lot, just follow these steps in order. Most of the value comes from the first two.

  1. Get health cover for the family first — this protects everyone against the most common big emergency.
  2. Add a term plan if anyone depends on your income, using the 10–15× rule of thumb.
  3. Don't over-insure or buy products you don't understand — more policies is not the same as more safety.
  4. Review your cover once a year, and after big life changes like marriage, a child, or a new home loan.
  5. Always read what is NOT covered — the exclusions tell you when the policy won't pay, which is just as important as when it will.

REMEMBER

Protect first, invest second. Term insurance and health insurance are the foundation everything else in your money plan sits on. Get them in place, then let your investments grow with peace of mind.
